What it does

A household that runs itself, out loud.

You describe how your home actually works once. After that FamilyOS carries it: it remembers, it asks, it checks, it pays, and it tells you only what genuinely needs a parent.

◷

Holds the schedule

Daily, weekly and every-other-Tuesday routines per person — with the cadence, the reset time and the fair share already worked out.

◌

Makes the ask

Reminders come from the app, at the time you chose, in a voice that never sighs. Nobody has to raise their voice to be heard.

▣

Checks the work

A kid snaps a photo; the on-device model reads it and gives an honest read. You confirm with one tap, or let it settle itself.

◆

Pays out on time

Allowance, screen time, privileges, a trip to the pool — whatever's real in your house. Earned is earned, paid automatically.

◍

Keeps the record

Who did what, when, and what it was worth. No more relying on memory, and no more litigating last Thursday.

♥

Escalates to a human

If a kid asks for help, gets stuck, or something sounds serious, it stops managing and gets you. That path is free on every plan.

The shield

Put something between the ask and the relationship.

Most household conflict isn't about the dishes. It's about who had to ask, how many times, and what tone it came out in by the third time. FamilyOS takes that job — so the pressure has somewhere to land that isn't the two of you.

Without it
The reminder comes from you, and it arrives carrying every other time you've said it.
Following up reads as distrust. Not following up reads as it didn't matter.
Rewards and consequences get renegotiated in the worst possible moment.
You end the day as the enforcer, even on a day that went fine.
With FamilyOS
The reminder comes from the app. It has no memory of yesterday's argument and no edge in its voice.
Verification is a photo, not an interrogation.
The terms were agreed in a calm moment and don't move when tempers do.
You get to be the one who notices the win: "I heard today went great."
ACE · the family companion
Meet ACE

When ACE says "time to clean your room," it isn't mom or dad saying it. No tone, no accumulated frustration, no score being settled. Kids can push back at a companion without pushing back at a parent — and because it stays consistent and fair every single time, the pushing back tends to fade.

To your kid: "That's your room done — +20 toward the pool trip. Want to leave the trash for after dinner?"
To you: "Room is done and logged. Leo's had a rough afternoon; I've moved the trash to tomorrow."

ACE only gives, never takes — nothing already earned is ever removed as a punishment.

Trauma-informed by design

Built for kids who've learned to expect the worst.

Foster and adoptive parents, kinship carers and families raising neurodivergent kids shaped this. If a system is unpredictable, shaming, or punitive, it doesn't work for those kids — so those things aren't options here.

Predictability

The rules don't move.

The same ask, at the same time, in the same words. Change is announced in advance, never sprung.

Never punitive

Nothing earned is taken back.

There is no mechanic anywhere in FamilyOS that removes stars, levels or progress. Loss isn't a lever we hand you.

No shame

No leaderboards, no comparison.

Kids see their own progress and one shared family goal — never a ranking against a sibling.

Agency

Choice wherever it's real.

Kids choose their order, their pace, their look, and can say "not today" without it becoming a confrontation.

Hard days

Low-demand mode.

Dysregulated day? Drop to the two things that truly matter. Streaks pause instead of breaking.

Repair

A missed day just ends.

Nothing accumulates into a debt or a lecture. Tomorrow opens clean, every time.

Your adventure

Your family's story, in your family's words.

A story is optional scaffolding, not the product. Turn it all the way up for a seven-year-old, turn it all the way off for a fifteen-year-old, and rename anything that doesn't sound like your house.

✎Write your own. Describe the world your kid is actually into — the Custom Adventure Engine builds the language around your real routines.
◑Or none at all. Plain mode: real names, real times, no characters. Same schedule, same payouts.
⇄Per person, not per household. One kid can be deep in a story while their sibling gets a clean checklist.
◈Rewards you actually give. Screen time, allowance, a later bedtime Friday, choosing dinner — you define what things are worth.

FAQ

Fair questions.

My kid isn't into games. Is this still for us?

Yes. The story layer is optional and per person — plain mode is real names, real times, real amounts, no characters. The scheduling, verification, payouts and escalation all work identically with it switched off.

Isn't this just bribing my kids?

It's the allowance and privileges you were already giving, made predictable and visible. Nothing is ever taken away as a punishment, and there's no mechanic for withholding what a kid already earned — so it doesn't run on fear or leverage.

What does "trauma-informed" mean here, concretely?

Six things we won't build: unpredictable rules, loss of earned progress, sibling leaderboards, shame-based nudges, accumulating debt for missed days, and a paywall between a child and a caregiver. Plus low-demand mode for dysregulated days. It isn't therapy — it's software that avoids the patterns that make routines unsafe for a kid.

Does the AI see photos of my kids?

Yes — on your hardware, in your house. Photos are checked by a model running on the Hub or your own computer. Images never leave your home network, and nothing is used for training.

Does it work for neurodivergent kids?

That's much of who it was built with. Fixed cadences, one visible next step, no time pressure on a task once started, low-demand days, and language you control word-for-word so a routine reads the way your kid needs it to.

What do I need to run it?

Either the $399 FamilyOS Hub — which includes six months of FamilyOS and arrives ready to plug in — or any reasonably modern computer you already own. Same software either way; the Hub just adds always-on display mode and a voice server.

What ages does it work for?

Built for kids 6–15, and it grows down as well as up: a six-year-old gets pictures and a companion, a fifteen-year-old gets a clean list and their allowance balance. Adults can be on it too.

Is it priced per child?

Never. One flat rate for the whole household — $19.99/mo or $199/yr — no matter how many people are on it. There are no tiers, no member limits, and nothing to upgrade to.

What happens when the beta ends?

Beta is free. At GA you choose monthly or annual, with a discounted first year as a beta family, and there's a 14-day trial before anything is charged. Hardware bought during beta carries a 30-day return window.

Can I leave?

Anytime, effective at the end of your billing period. Your family's data is exportable for 30 days after — it's yours, not ours.
